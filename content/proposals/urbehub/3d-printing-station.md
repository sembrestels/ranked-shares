## Why this matters

Hardware projects at the hub stall at the same point: the firmware works, the board works, and the case never gets made. Ordering prints online costs days and money for parts that should take an hour. A printer in the space turns "I'll print it at some point" into "I printed it tonight".

A shared printer also brings people in. Prints are visible, tangible and easy to talk about, and a printing corner is a natural first task for a new member who does not yet write Solidity.

## What we will buy

- One enclosed CoreXY printer with auto bed levelling (Bambu Lab P1S class), spare nozzles and build plates
- A starter stock of PLA and PETG, plus one roll of TPU, in the hub's colours
- A lockable cabinet for filament with silica gel, tools, deburring knives and calipers
- A dedicated shelf with a small fume filter and a fire-safe base

## Rules of use

- The printer is free for members; guests pay filament at cost
- Prints are queued on the hub board, longest jobs run overnight
- Two members act as maintainers; they run the calibration print each month

## Milestones

### A - Printer running - 1,100 EURC

- [ ] Printer, plates, nozzles and filament stock bought and installed on a dedicated shelf
- [ ] Calibration print and a first hub-branded print published in the hub chat
- [ ] Cabinet, tools and safety base in place

### B - Onboarding - 300 EURC

- [ ] One evening workshop: design a part in a free CAD tool, slice it, print it, with at least 10 attendees
- [ ] A one-page guide on the hub wiki: how to queue, slice, load filament, and who to call when it clogs
- [ ] Two named maintainers and a monthly maintenance checklist
