# Sealed seat ballots: DON-attested public tally, Noir proof of the sealed side

Status: draft 2026-09-05, revision 2. Alternative to
`2026-09-04-sealed-seat-ballots-design.md` (the ZisK version). Same product, same
contracts up to the tally, same CRE workflow with one more output. What changes is the
split of trust: the public half of PB-EAR is computed by the CRE workflow, attested by
the DON and auditable by anyone from chain data; the sealed half is proven with Noir
circuits in the coordinator's browser against a transcript the workflow publishes.
Sections identical to the ZisK spec say so and are not repeated.

## Context

Why not the ZisK design (see the conversation of 2026-09-05):

- **No proving machine.** ZisK needs a 32 GB Linux box the operator controls, because
  the witness holds the tallier key. bb.js proves Noir circuits in a browser tab, so the
  sealed side is proven from the coordinator's own page and the key never leaves it.
- **Public voters in the thousands.** Direct votes cost a dollar and a sybil can cast as
  many as it likes. PB-EAR gives sybils no extra influence (weight is money), but every
  public voter costs work in a tally. A circuit pays for its worst case, so public voters
  cannot live inside a fixed-size Noir circuit; a zkVM could hold them but brings the
  machine back. They are public data, so they need no proof at all: the DON computes
  them, anyone can recompute them, and the contract only stores the outcome.
- **No per-circuit ceremony**, and a path to MPC on the sealed side with coNoir. Not
  done here; kept open.

The split rests on one property of PB-EAR: inside a step, support is a sum, and with
the public entries in one contiguous block ahead of the sealed ones, the weight the
cumulative rounding deducts from the public block depends only on the block's total.
So the two halves can be computed by different parties if they share, per step, the
public support vector, the chosen project and its total support. That shared record is
the **transcript**. The tally itself stays one election under one rule; only the
computation is split. Running the public voters first as their own election and adding
the sealed ones afterwards would be a different rule and is not what this does.

What the split costs, stated once and repeated in B10: the transcript reveals, for each
funded project, how much sealed weight supported it at the moment it was funded. Never
per voter, never for unfunded projects. It is more than "only the funded set", which the
ZisK spec promised. And the public half of a `Proven` result rests on DON consensus over
a computation anyone can check, not on a proof.

### Decisions taken in this draft (each one can be reversed)

1. **Proof system:** Noir + Barretenberg UltraHonk, zero-knowledge flavour, Solidity
   verifiers generated by `bb write_solidity_verifier`. `nargo` and `bb` pinned; a
   version bump regenerates both verifiers and means a new pool.
2. **Only sealed voters are capped.** The profile caps addresses with a sealed
   ciphertext (`N_SEALED_MAX`, default 256, enforced at `voteSealed`). Direct voters are
   unbounded; the only lever against sybils is a minimum direct contribution to cast a
   public ballot (`minDirectVote`, constructor parameter, suggested 10 USDC).
3. **Encryption:** ECDH on Grumpkin with a Poseidon2 mask; a ballot packs into one
   field element; a ciphertext is three field elements stored on-chain. No log scan.
4. **Commitment:** in `close`, a keccak chain over public entries (only the workflow
   recomputes it) and a Poseidon2 chain over sealed entries with a checkpoint every
   `B` entries (the circuits recompute it). Weights are 64-bit, capped on-chain.
5. **Transcript from the enclave.** The confidential workflow already runs the whole
   tally to produce the provisional result; it now also emits the transcript. The
   contract stores its Poseidon2 chain hash and emits the data.
6. **Two circuits, one state hash, on-chain chaining, no recursion.** `ingest` decrypts
   a batch of sealed voters; `tally` replays `K` transcript steps on the sealed state.
   Proofs are verified one by one by `advance`. Default profile: `N_SEALED_MAX = 256`,
   `M_MAX = 16`, `B = 32`, `K = 8`: at most twelve proofs per round.
7. **The master secret is a wallet signature**, regenerated in the browser and uploaded
   once to the Vault DON. No secret file exists.
8. **`Proven` means:** sealed side proven cryptographically against the committed
   ciphertexts and the pool's key; public side attested by the DON and reproducible by
   anyone from chain data. The README says so in those words.

Unchanged from the ZisK spec and not repeated: the product decisions of its context,
A1 contracts and `PoolBase` hooks, A3 seat ledger, A5 phases and finality, A6.5 grace
paths, A6.6 claims, and the Arc / CRE facts. Target chain and forwarder are the same.

## Part B: Specification

### B1. Contracts

```
Paths are relative to `src/`; the variant's own contracts live under `src/noir/`.

PBEAR.sol               unchanged; still the engine of RankedShares and the oracle every
                        port is tested against; not used by the sealed pool
PoolBase.sol            as in A1, plus the 64-bit caps of B2
RankedShares.sol        as in A1
noir/NoirRankedShares.sol  is PoolBase, IReceiver: ballots, commitment, transcript report,
                        proof chain (B6)
noir/lib/Poseidon2.sol  BN254 Poseidon2, t = 4, sponge with rate 3, byte-for-byte the
                        Barretenberg FieldSponge that bb.js `poseidon2Hash` computes and
                        that `reference/noir/poseidon2.py` mirrors (spike B12.2). The Noir
                        circuits implement the same sponge over the
                        `poseidon2_permutation` black box; the stdlib's comptime
                        `Poseidon2Hasher` is not that sponge and is not used.
noir/lib/Grumpkin.sol   on-curve check y² = x³ − 17 over the BN254 scalar field
noir/verifiers/IngestVerifier.sol   generated by bb, committed, one per profile
noir/verifiers/TallyVerifier.sol    generated by bb, committed, one per profile
```

Constructor parameters beyond `PoolBase`'s: `tallierPkX`, `tallierPkY`, `keySalt`,
`forwarder`, `coordinator` (the address allowed to restart the tally chain, B6.5),
`ingestVerifier`, `tallyVerifier`, `profile` (`nSealedMax`, `mMax`, `batch`),
`minDirectVote`, `minSealedVote`, `proofGrace`, `abandonGrace` (both at most 365 days).
All immutable; `poseidon` and both verifiers must have code. `profileId()` returns
`keccak256(abi.encode(nSealedMax, mMax, batch))` so clients can refuse a pool whose
verifiers were built for another profile.

### B2. Voter identity, weights and limits

As A2 with these changes:

```
mapping(address => uint256) internal _directBallot;   // packed ranks (B4), valid iff hasDirect
mapping(address => bool)    public  hasDirect;
mapping(address => uint256[3]) internal _sealed;      // (R.x, R.y, c); R.x == 0 means none
uint256 public sealedCount;                           // addresses with a ciphertext
```

Limits enforced at write time, all reverting:

- `addProject`: `cost < 2^64` (`CostTooLarge`), `projectCount < mMax`
  (`TooManyProjects`).
- every deposit path: `totalWeight ≤ 2^64 − 1` after the deposit (`WeightOverflow`).
- `vote`: `directWeight[msg.sender] ≥ minDirectVote` and non-zero (`BelowMinimumVote`);
  a zero-weight ballot would only enlarge `voters`.
- `voteSealed`: `seatWeight[msg.sender] ≥ minSealedVote` and non-zero (`NoSeatWeight`),
  so filling the sealed slots costs real capital, as `minDirectVote` does for the public
  side; first ciphertext of an address: `sealedCount < nSealedMax`
  (`TooManySealedVoters`). Replacements do not count.

Voter registration itself is unbounded.

**Tally entry order.** The tally sees, in this order: first every registered address
with a direct ballot, in registration order, as `(addr, directWeight, directBallot)`;
then every address with a sealed ciphertext, in registration order, as `(addr,
seatWeight, sealedBallot)`. The first block is the **public block**, the second the
**sealed block**. An entry without a usable ballot has weight zero and its weight is
abstaining. `pbear.ts`, the transcript and the `tally` circuit all fix this order; it
is what makes the cumulative rounding of the public block independent of the sealed
one.

### B4. Ballots

**Packing.** A ballot `ranks[0..m)` (one byte per project, same meaning as today) is the
field element `packed = Σ ranks[c] · 256^c`. With `mMax ≤ 31` this is below `2^248`.
Unpacking and the competition-ranking check are defined on the bytes and are identical
in Solidity, TypeScript and Noir.

**Direct:** `vote(bytes ranks)` as A4 plus the minimum of B2; stores `packed`, sets
`hasDirect`.

**Sealed:** `voteSealed(uint256 rx, uint256 ry, uint256 c)` — `inPhase(Open)`,
`beforeDeadline`, `seatWeight[msg.sender] > 0` (`NoSeatWeight`), `rx != 0` and
`(rx, ry)` on Grumpkin (`InvalidCiphertext`), all three below the field modulus, cap of
B2. Stores the triple, replacing any previous one, emits `SealedVote(address indexed
voter)`. The on-curve check is not optional: an off-curve `R` would make `ingest`
unsatisfiable and stall the pool (B7, "every input must be provable").

Gas: three storage slots, about 66k the first time and 9k for a replacement.

**Encryption** (frontend, enclave and circuit share one definition):

```
curve      Grumpkin (base field = BN254 scalar field, so points are native in Noir)
pk         tallier public key (x, y), immutable in the pool
k          fresh random Grumpkin scalar per vote
R          k·G
S          k·pk
mask       Poseidon2([DOMAIN, S.x, S.y, voter address as field])
           DOMAIN = the ASCII bytes "RankedShares/sealed/v2" read as a big-endian integer
c          packed + mask   (mod field)
ciphertext (R.x, R.y, c)
```

Decryption: `S = sk·R`, recompute `mask`, `packed = c − mask`, unpack. A ballot is
absent if `packed ≥ 256^m`, any byte exceeds `m`, or the competition-ranking check
fails. A random field element passes with negligible probability, so a ciphertext
copied from another voter or another pool decrypts to an absent ballot. No MAC, for the
reasons of A4.

**Tallier key.** `masterSecret = keccak256(sig)`, `sig` the operator EOA's
`personal_sign` over the ASCII message `RankedShares tallier master secret v1`
(deterministic per RFC 6979; contract wallets cannot be operators).
`sk = keccak256(masterSecret ‖ keySalt) mod q`, `q` the Grumpkin group order,
`pk = sk·G`. Deploy script, enclave and browser prover share the derivation. The circuit
receives `sk` as two 128-bit limbs and checks `sk·G == pk`.

### B6. Closing, results, finalisation

**B6.1 Commitment.** `close(uint256 maxVoters)` — `inPhase(Closing)`, anyone,
repeatable, balance check on the first call as A6.1. Walks `voters` from `closeCursor`,
maintaining two chains:

```
if hasDirect[a]:
    hPub = keccak256(abi.encodePacked(hPub, a, directWeight[a], _directBallot[a]))
if _sealed[a][0] != 0:
    j = sealedCursor++                                     // index in the sealed block
    hSealed = Poseidon2([hSealed, uint160(a), seatWeight[a], rx, ry, c])
    if ((j + 1) % batch == 0 || j + 1 == sealedCount) checkpoint[(j + batch) / batch] = hSealed
```

Both chains start at zero; `checkpoint[0] = 0`. With no sealed voters `checkpoint[1]`
stays zero, which is exactly what an ingest proof over an empty batch produces. When the
cursor reaches the end:

```
costsHash   = Poseidon2(costs[0..m))                       // m elements, no padding
inputsRoot  = keccak256(abi.encodePacked(hPub, hSealed, sealedCount, costsHash, totalWeight))
numBatches  = max(1, ceil(sealedCount / batch))
closed = true; emit Closed(inputsRoot, sealedCount)
```

When the walk ends, the number of sealed entries absorbed must equal `sealedCount`
(`SealedCountMismatch`): the invariant that every address with a ciphertext is in
`voters` holds because `voteSealed` needs seat weight and a seat grant registers, but
`close` asserts it rather than trusting three hops of reasoning.

`inputsRoot` binds the DON report. The proof chain binds to the checkpoints,
`costsHash` and the transcript hash instead (B6.4). Chain id and pool address are not
hashed; a proof is only ever checked against the storage of the pool it is sent to.

Gas: one keccak per public voter, about 5k; one Poseidon2 sponge per sealed voter,
expected in the tens of thousands (spike B12.2). Chunked across calls.

**B6.2 Reports from the DON.** As A6.2, with the `RESULT` payload extended:

```
abi.encode(bytes32 inputsRoot, uint256[] fundedOrder, uint256[] transcript)
```

`transcript` is the flat word list of B6.3. Accepted in `Tally` only, once;
`inputsRoot` must match (`InputMismatch`); `fundedOrder` valid as A6.2; the transcript
must parse (`InvalidTranscript`): a whole number of steps, `m + 3` words each, at most
`2m` steps, `best` either a project id or `NONE = 2^64 − 1`, and the projects with a
non-`NONE` `best` in order must equal `fundedOrder`. The contract computes
`transcriptHash` as the Poseidon2 chain of B6.3 and stores it, stores `provisional`, sets
`resultReported = true`,
emits `ProvisionalResult(fundedOrder)` and `Transcript(uint256[] transcript)`.

Hashing the transcript on-chain costs one Poseidon2 sponge of `m + 4` elements per
step, up to `2m` steps: measured at about 400k gas per step (Poseidon2 costs about 54k gas per
permutation on the EVM even with unrolled rounds), so a worst-case 32-step transcript
at the default profile is about 13 M gas in one transaction; the kind-3 chunked
delivery below is the fallback if Arc's block limit requires it (B12.4, B12.7). If the
report size or that gas turns out to be a problem (spike B12.4), the fallback is a
kind-3 `TRANSCRIPT` report delivered in chunks with the running hash carried in
storage.

**Amendment (2026-09-05): report authorisation.** `msg.sender == forwarder` alone is not
enough. Chainlink's KeystoneForwarder is a per-chain singleton shared by every workflow
registered with it, so any other workflow owner could deliver a kind-1 report to this
pool — writing `provisional`/`transcriptHash`, starting the `proofGrace` clock and, once
it elapsed, letting `acceptProvisional` finalise a forged result (`inputsRoot` is public,
so the payload is easy to forge). `onReport` therefore also checks the metadata the
forwarder prepends — `abi.encodePacked(bytes32 workflowId, bytes10 workflowName, address
workflowOwner)`, optionally followed by a `bytes2 reportId` — against the immutables
`workflowOwner` and `workflowName` taken from `Config`: shorter than 62 bytes is
`BadMetadata`, a mismatch at bytes 42..62 (owner) or, when `workflowName != bytes10(0)`,
at 32..42 (name) is `WrongWorkflow`. The check runs before the report is decoded and is
shared with the cre pool as `checkWorkflow` in `src/lib/CreMetadata.sol`. It binds a
report to a workflow owner, not to a workflow build — `workflowId` is not checked, so
that owner may redeploy code under the same name and still report; that is the trust
boundary. `workflowOwner == address(0)` disables the check, for `cre workflow simulate`'s
MockForwarder (which sends no metadata) only: such a pool must never hold real funds, and
`script/DeployNoir.s.sol` refuses to deploy one without `ALLOW_ANY_WORKFLOW=1`.

**B6.3 Transcript.** One record per executed step `t = 0, 1, …`, including the
terminal one:

```
step_t = [level, pubSupport[0], …, pubSupport[m − 1], best, total]
```

- `level`: the rank level the step ran at.
- `pubSupport[c]`: the weight of the public block approving `c` at `level`, zero for
  funded projects, with the public weights as they stand at the start of the step.
- `best`: the project funded by the step, or `NONE` when nothing was eligible (the step
  either advanced the level or, at `level ≥ m`, ended the tally). A step at which the
  tally is already exhausted is not recorded: exhaustion is checked before the step, as
  in `_startTally` and at the end of `PBEAR.step()`.
- `total`: `pubSupport[best] + sealedSupport[best]`, or zero when `best = NONE`.

Hash chain: `tHash_0 = 0`, `tHash_{t+1} = Poseidon2([tHash_t, step_t…])` with `m + 4`
elements. `transcriptHash = tHash_S` for `S` recorded steps.

What a reader can verify from public data alone: every `pubSupport` vector, given the
sequence of `(level, best, total)` (replay the public block, deducting
`mulDiv(cum, cost, total)` differences to the supporters of `best` after each funding);
that the funded set matches; that `total ≥ cost[best]`; and that no public support
alone exceeded a cost in a `NONE` step. What only the sealed proof can verify: that
`best` was the argmax with the sealed block counted, and that `total` is right.
`prover/cli audit` performs the public replay (B8).

**B6.4 Proof public inputs.** `bytes32[]` in circuit declaration order.

`ingest` (one per batch `k`):

```
[k, nSealed, m, budget, pk.x, pk.y, hIn, hOut, stateIn, stateOut]
```

`tally` (one per group of `K` steps):

```
[costsHash, stateIn, stateOut, done, tHashOut, fundedCount, fundedOrderPacked]
```

`fundedOrderPacked = Σ fundedOrder[j] · 256^j` over the first `fundedCount` entries.

**B6.5 `advance(bytes proof, bytes32[] publicInputs, bool restart)`** — `inPhase(Tally)`,
`finality == None`, anyone. Storage: `ingestCursor`, `stateCommit` (zero until the
first proof), `ingestedState` (the commitment after the last ingest batch).

Ingest branch, while `ingestCursor < numBatches`:

1. Require `publicInputs[0..6] == [ingestCursor, sealedCount, m, totalWeight, pk.x,
   pk.y, checkpoint[ingestCursor]]` and `publicInputs[8] == stateCommit`
   (`ProofOutOfOrder` / `InputMismatch`).
2. Require `publicInputs[7] == checkpoint[ingestCursor + 1]` (`InputMismatch`): every
   batch is checked against the chain the moment it arrives.
3. `IngestVerifier.verify(proof, publicInputs)` must return true (`InvalidProof`).
4. `stateCommit = publicInputs[9]; ingestCursor++`; on the last batch
   `ingestedState = stateCommit`. Emit `Ingested(k)`.

Tally branch, otherwise; requires that a kind-1 report has been accepted
(`resultReported`, a boolean set in B6.2; `TranscriptPending` otherwise). The flag exists
because `transcriptHash` is legitimately zero for a pool exhausted before its first step,
whose honest transcript is empty:

1. Require `publicInputs[0] == costsHash`, `publicInputs[1] == stateCommit`.
2. `TallyVerifier.verify(proof, publicInputs)` must return true.
3. `stateCommit = publicInputs[2]`. Emit `Advanced()`.
4. If `publicInputs[3] == 1`: require `publicInputs[4] == transcriptHash`
   (`TranscriptMismatch`), unpack `fundedOrder`, require it equals `provisional`
   (`ResultMismatch`; the transcript determines it, so a difference is a bug), set
   `funded[id]`, `spent`, `finality = Proven`, emit `Finalized(Proven, fundedOrder)`.

`restart` is honoured in the tally branch only and only from `coordinator`
(`NotCoordinator` otherwise): when true, `stateCommit` is reset to `ingestedState`
before step 1, and `TallyRestarted()` is emitted once the proof has been accepted. The
restriction is necessary: proofs are public once submitted, so without it anyone could
replay the first tally group's proof with `restart = true` and rewind the chain at will,
censoring an honest `Proven` until the grace path applies. Forward `advance` calls stay
permissionless. The verifier call is wrapped so that a verifier that reverts on a bad
proof still surfaces as `InvalidProof`. The ingest chain is a deterministic
function of committed inputs and a key tied to `pk`, so it never needs restarting; the
tally chain can go wrong only if the prover fed a transcript slice that does not match
the DON's, which surfaces at step 4, and this lets an honest prover start over.

**Amendment (2026-09-05): `advanceMany(bytes[] proofs, bytes32[][] publicInputs, bool
restart)`** — the same `inPhase(Tally)` entry, verifying several proofs of the chain in
one transaction so a coordinator confirms once in a wallet and pays one transaction
instead of one per proof. `proofs.length == publicInputs.length` (`InputMismatch`), a
non-empty batch (`EmptyBatch`), and then element `i` in order, routed exactly as
`advance` routes it — ingest while `ingestCursor < numBatches`, tally afterwards — so
every rule above applies unchanged to each element. Two things are the batch's own:
`restart` is applied to the first *tally* element of the batch and never to an ingest one
(a batch that still has ingest pending is a forward run of the whole chain), still only
from `coordinator`; and an element after the finalising `done` proof reverts with
`WrongPhase`, since `inPhase` only sees the phase the call started in. The batch is
atomic — one rejected proof reverts all of it — which is what makes it safe to send a
whole chain speculatively. `advance` keeps its exact behaviour; both it and the loop
route through one internal helper.

A transcript the sealed side cannot satisfy (the enclave lied or has a bug) produces no
proof; then `acceptProvisional` after `proofGrace` applies the DON result on DON trust,
as A6.5, and the coordinator's audit log is the evidence something is off. Turning that
into an on-chain dispute is a follow-up (B11).

Verifiers as in the previous revision: bb-generated, split in two contracts because of
the 24 KB limit, deployed once per chain per profile by `script/DeployNoirVerifiers.s.sol`,
BN254 precompiles from Reth. **Measured (B12.3), local EVM against the generated
verifiers, real `bb` proofs (`RealProofsTest`):** `advance` costs about 686k–930k gas
per call, one ingest or tally group per proof (test profile fixtures) — not yet
confirmed on Arc testnet.

**B6.6 Grace paths, B6.7 Claims.** As A6.5 and A6.6, with two amendments. The clock
for `acceptProvisional` starts when the kind-1 report is accepted (`reportedAt`,
recorded in B6.2), not at the deadline: `acceptProvisional` requires
`block.timestamp >= reportedAt + proofGrace`, so the prover always gets the full grace
window after the transcript exists, however late `close` or the report were. `abandon`
keeps the deadline anchor (`votingDeadline + abandonGrace`), since it is the path for a
DON that never reported. Both sums are computed in 256-bit arithmetic; the grace values
are `uint64` and must never overflow the comparison.

### B7. Circuits (`noir/`)

```
noir/pbear/    library: ballot packing, validation, one transcript-driven step, state hash
noir/sealed/   library: key check, ECDH, mask, decrypt
noir/ingest/   binary circuit
noir/tally/    binary circuit
noir/profile/  one Nargo.toml per profile setting N_SEALED_MAX, M_MAX, B, K as globals
noir/tests/    fixtures shared with Foundry, vitest and the Python reference
```

**State.** `E = N_SEALED_MAX` entries, sealed block only.

```
State {
  weights: [u64; E], ballots: [Field; E],          // packed; 0 only when ingest found no valid ballot
  funded: [bool; M_MAX], funded_order: [u8; M_MAX], funded_count: u8,
  level: u8, spent: u64, done: bool, m: u8, budget: u64, t_hash: Field
}
```

`commit(state) = Poseidon2` over the flat array `[weights…, ballots…, fundedBits,
fundedOrderPacked, funded_count, level, spent, done, m, budget, t_hash]`. The empty
state has all weights zero, `level = 1`, `done = false`, `t_hash = 0`, `m` and `budget`
from the public inputs. A weight can reach zero by deduction, or be zero because a seat
was transferred away after the ballot was cast, while the ballot stays in place; entries
with weight zero never influence a step, and nothing ever zeroes a ballot after ingest,
so the commitment stays reproducible by the reference.

**`ingest(k)`.** Private: `sk` limbs, `state_in` in full, the `B` sealed entries of the
batch (`addr, seatWeight, rx, ry, c`), all-zero past `nSealed`. Mask-based, no early
exit:

1. `assert sk·G == pk`.
2. If `k == 0` assert `stateIn == 0` and use the empty state, else assert
   `commit(state_in) == stateIn`.
3. `h = hIn`; for slot `j`, global index `i = k·B + j`: `h' = Poseidon2([h, addr,
   seatWeight, rx, ry, c])`, `h = if i < nSealed { h' } else { h }`.
4. Decrypt: `S = sk·R` (a zero `rx` is swapped for the generator before the black box
   and discarded after), `mask`, `packed`, validity flags. Entry `i` gets
   `(seatWeight if valid else 0, packed if valid else 0)`; slots past `nSealed` get
   zeros.
5. `assert hOut == h`, `assert stateOut == commit(state)`.

Every input the contract can hold must be provable: the on-curve check happens in
`voteSealed`, malformed plaintext becomes an absent ballot through selects, no `assert`
depends on voter-chosen data. An unprovable input would let one voter stall a pool.

**`tally`.** Private: costs (`M_MAX`, zero past `m`), `state_in` in full, `K`
transcript steps (`level, pubSupport[M_MAX], best, total`, zero-filled past the end).
`assert Poseidon2(costs[0..m)) == costsHash`, `assert commit(state_in) == stateIn`,
then `K` times `state = step(state, costs, step_t)`, expose `done`, `t_hash`,
`funded_count`, `fundedOrderPacked`, `assert stateOut == commit(state)`.

`step`, mirroring `PBEAR.step()` with the sealed block only:

```
if done                       → unchanged
else if exhausted(state)      → done = true                       (nothing absorbed)
else
  assert step.level == level
  t_hash = Poseidon2([t_hash, level, pubSupport…, best, total])
  sealed[c] = Σ_e weights[e] · [ rank(ballots[e], c) ≤ level ] · [ !funded[c] ]
  sum[c]    = pubSupport[c] + sealed[c]                              (unfunded c)
  argmax    = over unfunded c with sum[c] ≥ cost[c]: highest sum, then lowest cost,
              then lowest id; NONE if no c qualifies
  assert best == argmax
  if best == NONE:
      assert total == 0
      if level ≥ m → done = true  else level += 1
  else:
      assert total == sum[best]
      cum = pubSupport[best]                      // the public block came first
      for e: if entry e supports best:
          newCum = cum + w
          deduct = mulDiv(newCum, thr, total) − mulDiv(cum, thr, total)
          weights[e] = w − deduct; cum = newCum
      funded[best] = true; funded_order[funded_count++] = best; spent += thr
      if exhausted(state) → done = true
```

`rank(ballot, c)` unpacks byte `c` and applies the default rank `1 + number of
non-zero ranks`, as `_setBallot` does. Two things the circuit must do that the
reference gets for free from Python integers: range-check every `pubSupport[c]` and
`total` of a step to 64 bits before using them (a `Field` carries no such bound and the
`mulDiv` hint relies on it), and absorb exactly `m + 4` elements into the transcript
hash, never `M_MAX + 4`, because Poseidon2's capacity encodes the input length and the
contract hashes `m` support entries. `mulDiv` is the unconstrained-hint pattern:
the product of two `u64` fits the field, the hint returns `(q, r)`, the circuit asserts
`q·d + r == a·b` and `r < d`. The asserts on `best` and `total` are what turn the DON's
transcript into a claim the proof checks; the asserts on `level` and the hash chain
are what make a forged slice fail at `advance` step 4.

Cost per step at the default profile: `E · M_MAX` rank comparisons and `E` divisions,
roughly 90k gates, plus one sponge of `m + 4`; eight steps plus two state commitments
of about 530 elements per proof, under a million gates. Ingest: 32 scalar
multiplications and sponges plus two commitments, a few hundred thousand.

**Build and artifacts.** `nargo compile` per profile, `bb write_vk`,
`bb write_solidity_verifier`, outputs committed under `noir/artifacts/<profile>/` and
`src/noir/verifiers/`. ZK flavour of UltraHonk for proving and verifier generation.

### B8. Browser prover (`prover/`)

```
prover/src/core/    fetch pool state (viem), decrypt, replay, build witnesses, prove
                    (bb.js), submit
prover/src/web/     coordinator page (Vite): connect wallet, sign for the master secret,
                    prove batch by batch with progress, send `advance` per proof
prover/src/cli/     same core under Node: CI, fixtures, headless operator, and `audit`
```

Coordinator page, in `Tally`:

1. Read voters, weights, ballots, ciphertexts, costs, `totalWeight`, `keySalt`,
   checkpoints, `ingestCursor`, `stateCommit`, and the `Transcript` event if present.
2. Sign, derive `sk`, check `sk·G` against the pool's `pk`; refuse on mismatch.
3. Decrypt locally and run `pbear.ts` over both blocks in the order of B2, producing
   the coordinator's own transcript. If the DON's transcript exists and differs, stop
   and show the first differing step: this is the audit that catches a wrong enclave.
   If it does not exist yet, ingest proofs can still be produced and sent.
4. For each remaining ingest batch, then once the transcript is on-chain, for each
   group of `K` steps until `done`: build the witness, prove with bb.js (WASM,
   multi-threaded; the page needs COOP/COEP headers), send `advance`, wait for
   inclusion. Resumable from `ingestCursor` and `stateCommit`.
5. Show `Finalized(Proven)`.

`prover/cli audit --pool <addr>` needs no key: it replays the public block against the
on-chain transcript as B6.3 describes and prints the first inconsistency, if any.
Anyone can run it; it is the "auditable by anyone" of decision 8 made concrete.

Key and plaintext live in page memory only. bb.js fetches the reference string from
Aztec's CDN on first use.

Expected time per proof in a browser: tens of seconds to a couple of minutes at the
default profile (B12.1) — still an estimate; the browser half of B12.1 is plan 4's
spike. The native half is measured (`noir/README.md`): `bb prove` on the default
profile takes 1.13 s / 243 MB peak for `ingest` and 9.28 s / 1.73 GB peak for `tally`,
well under the two-minute / four-gigabyte ceiling, so `K` stays 8 (decision 6
unchanged). At most twelve proofs, a wallet confirmation each unless the page uses a
session key.

**Evidence for submission:** `Finalized(Proven, …)` on arcscan for a pool with sealed
votes, a recording of the coordinator page producing the chain, and an `audit` run.

### B9. CRE workflow (`cre/`)

As A7, with these changes:

- Step 3 reads ciphertexts from storage (`sealedOf(addr)`); no log scan.
- Step 4, inside the enclave, builds the entry list in the order of B2, runs
  `pbear.ts` in transcript mode, and leaves the enclave with `(inputsRoot, fundedOrder,
  transcript)`. The transcript is derived from plaintext, and B6.3 defines exactly what
  it reveals; nothing else derived from plaintext leaves.
- Step 5 writes the extended kind-1 report (B6.2).
- `cre/src/sealed.ts` implements B4 on Grumpkin and Poseidon2 in pure TypeScript with
  no native or WASM dependency, so it runs under QuickJS: Grumpkin through
  noble-curves' generic short-Weierstrass constructor, Poseidon2 through a pure-TS
  implementation checked against Noir's vectors.
- `inputsRoot(...)` as B6.1 (keccak over the two chains); the sealed chain needs
  Poseidon2 in TypeScript too.
- Reads use `votersFrom(start, count)`, which returns addresses, direct weights, packed
  direct ballots, seat weights and ciphertexts for a page of `voters`, instead of five
  calls per voter.
- The Vault secret is `masterSecret` of B4, produced once by a script that asks the
  wallet for the signature and pipes the keccak into `cre secrets create` through an
  environment variable; nothing is written to disk.

`pbear.ts` gains a transcript-mode output and takes the public-first entry order. The
same function serves the enclave, the browser prover and the audit.

### B10. Gas, limits and trust

- `voteSealed`: about 66k first time, 9k replacement. `vote`: as today.
- `close`: about 5k per public voter, tens of thousands per sealed voter, chunked.
- `onReport` kind 1: transcript hashing, a few million gas once (B12.4).
- `advance`: one Honk verification, low millions, at most twelve per round.
- Sealed voters capped by the profile at `voteSealed`; public voters bounded only by
  `minDirectVote` and by what the enclave computes in its time budget (thousands of
  entries are milliseconds in TypeScript).

Trust summary, replacing A10:

| Party | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Public | see direct ballots, see who voted sealed and how often, replay the public half of the transcript, verify every proof | learn any sealed ranking or which seats abstained |
| Public, from the transcript | learn the sealed weight behind each funded project at the step it was funded, and the level | learn sealed support for unfunded projects, or anything per voter |
| Sponsor | see which members submitted a sealed ballot | see or infer rankings beyond what the transcript and funded set imply |
| DON nodes | see ciphertexts, public data, the funded set and the transcript | see the key or any plaintext |
| Enclave | see everything | publish a transcript the sealed proof will accept unless it is the honest one; change committed inputs |
| DON, collectively | make a wrong result `Attested` after `proofGrace` if no proof arrives | make a wrong result `Proven` |
| Operator (holds the signature) | decrypt off-chain | make a result `Proven` that differs from PB-EAR over the committed inputs and the DON's transcript; be stopped from decrypting (documented) |

`Proven` therefore means: sealed half proven, public half DON-attested and reproducible
by anyone with `audit`. The README states this and states which path a pool took.

### B11. Follow-ups, explicitly out of scope

- **On-chain dispute of the transcript.** Because the public half is reproducible, a
  challenger could re-execute one step with `PBEAR.sol` against a per-step commitment of
  public weights and slash a bond. This is why the Solidity engine stays. It would close
  the DON-trust gap on the public half without any prover.
- **Recursive aggregation** of the proof chain into one proof, on any untrusted machine.
- **MPC proving** of `ingest` with coNoir, splitting `sk`. Would remove the operator's
  ability to decrypt.
- **Sealed slots recycled through NFT seats.** `claimSeat` moves a seat's weight to a new
  holder while the old holder's ciphertext and `sealedCount` slot stay, so one seat's
  capital can fill several sealed slots. Decrementing `sealedCount` and clearing the
  ciphertext in `_onSeatRevoked` when the holder's seat weight reaches zero would close
  it; it contradicts B7's "ballots are never zeroed after ingest" only for entries that
  are removed before `close`, which the circuits never see.
- **Larger profiles** and a profile registry.

## What changed against the ZisK spec, in one table

| | ZisK spec | This spec |
|---|---|---|
| Who tallies the public block | the ZisK guest | the CRE enclave, DON-attested, anyone can replay |
| Who tallies the sealed block | the ZisK guest | Noir circuits in the coordinator's browser |
| What `Proven` covers | everything, cryptographically | sealed half cryptographically, public half by attestation plus audit |
| Prover machine | Linux, 32 GB, operator-controlled | none; a browser tab |
| Trusted setup | ZisK PLONK key | none per circuit |
| Encryption | secp256k1 + keccak stream | Grumpkin ECDH + Poseidon2 mask |
| Ciphertext | event bytes, keccak in storage | three field elements in storage |
| Commitment | one keccak chain | keccak chain (public) + Poseidon2 chain with checkpoints (sealed) |
| Weights | uint256 | uint64, capped on-chain |
| Voter limits | proving time | sealed capped by profile; public unbounded, `minDirectVote` |
| Extra public information | none | sealed weight behind each funded project, per step |
| Verifier gas | high 200k, once | low millions, up to twelve times |
| DON report | result | result + transcript |
